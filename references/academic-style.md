# Academic prose

Use patterns that fit the purpose of the passage. Work from the user's evidence and preserve the level of certainty.

- Abstract: state the research question, sample, and design in concrete terms. Give the main result and a specific limit on its interpretation.
- Methods: name what was measured and the unit of analysis. When multiple summaries are compared, describe the inclusion and weighting rules for each.
- Results: put values beside their group names and units. Describe direction and magnitude without introducing significance claims.
- Discussion: connect each inference to the observation behind it. When two analyses change more than one factor, describe the changes together and avoid attributing the result to just one.
- Match the supplied spelling convention, register and citation format. Treat any style sample as a model for organization and syntax, with the user's text supplying the facts.

[Cutting (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134913-00002031) illustrates reporting a sample, frame-sampling rule, and operational definition. [Bland and Altman (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(86)90837-8) illustrates opening with a measurement comparison and specifying what it evaluates.
